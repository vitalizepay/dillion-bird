Most businesses treat bookkeeping as the thing that has to be done before the tax return. That framing is why so many UAE companies spend January reconstructing a year they could have understood month by month.

Since Corporate Tax arrived, accurate records stopped being optional housekeeping and became the evidence base for what you report. This guide covers what proper bookkeeping involves here, what the FTA actually expects, and where businesses most often come unstuck. For the filing deadlines and thresholds themselves, see our [Dubai bookkeeping, tax and audit calendar](/blog/dubai-bookkeeping-tax-audit-compliance-calendar).

## Bookkeeping and Accounting Are Not the Same Thing

Bookkeeping is the systematic recording of what happened: sales and income, purchases and expenses, bank transactions, receivables and payables, payroll, fixed assets, inventory, loans and VAT transactions.

Accounting is what you do with that record. Interpreting it to understand profitability, cash position, financial health and where the business is actually making or losing money.

You cannot do the second without the first being right. A great deal of poor financial decision-making in small businesses traces back to management accounts built on records nobody reconciled.

## What the FTA Expects You to Hold

The Federal Tax Authority requires businesses subject to Corporate Tax to keep records supporting the information in their returns. That includes transaction records, asset registers, liability records and ownership information.

**FTA Decision No. 4 of 2026** sets rules for how accounting records and commercial books are maintained for VAT and Corporate Tax purposes. One requirement worth acting on now: under Article 3, where records are held as electronic copies or photocopies, they must be accessible to the Authority on request.

That sounds administrative until you consider how most small companies actually store documents. Invoices in a founder's personal email. Receipts in a phone gallery. A spreadsheet on one laptop. If the Authority asks and you cannot produce it in a usable form, the fact that the record technically exists somewhere is not much help.

Financial statements should be prepared under accounting standards accepted in the UAE, with IFRS the most commonly used.

## How Long to Keep Records: Seven Years, Not Five

This one trips people up because two different periods apply.

The **Tax Procedures framework** sets a minimum of **five years** after the end of the relevant tax period. **Corporate Tax** requires **seven years**. The longer period governs.

So a business that clears its archive at the five-year mark on the strength of the VAT rule creates a Corporate Tax violation for the remaining two years. Work to seven as standard. Longer periods can apply in circumstances such as audits or disputes.

## What Proper Bookkeeping Actually Covers

**Sales and income.** Customer invoices, receipts and credit notes recorded and classified correctly, not just totalled.

**Purchases and expenses.** Categorised properly and supported by documentation. Rent, utilities, software subscriptions, marketing, professional fees, travel, employee costs and supplier purchases each sit differently for tax purposes.

**Bank reconciliation.** Comparing your records against what the bank actually shows. This is where missing transactions, duplicates, unrecorded receipts and plain errors surface, and it is the single most skipped task in small-business finance.

**Receivables.** Outstanding invoices, overdue balances, customer payment history, expected collections. A profitable business with unmanaged receivables still runs out of cash.

**Payables.** What you owe suppliers and when, so payment can be planned rather than reacted to.

**Payroll.** Salary costs and employee-related obligations recorded accurately within the accounts.

**Fixed assets.** Equipment, vehicles, computers and machinery with a proper register behind them. The FTA has specifically identified asset registers among the records businesses may need for Corporate Tax.

**Inventory.** For trading, retail, manufacturing and e-commerce: opening stock, purchases, sales, closing stock, valuation, adjustments and cost of goods sold. Inventory errors distort both profit and tax.

## VAT: Traceable From Source to Return

A VAT-registered business needs its system to distinguish output VAT, input VAT, tax invoices, credit and debit notes, zero-rated and exempt transactions, imports and exports, and adjustments.

The requirement underneath all of it is traceability: the Authority should be able to follow a VAT amount from the source document through to the figure on the return. Where accounting records and VAT returns diverge, that gap is what an audit finds.

## The Reports Worth Looking At Monthly

Bookkeeping earns its cost when it becomes information you use:

- **Profit and loss** over the period
- **Balance sheet**: assets, liabilities, equity
- **Cash flow**: how money actually moved
- **Receivables ageing**: who owes you and how late they are
- **Payables**: what is due and when
- **VAT summary** before filing, not after

Monthly or quarterly management accounts turn these into something a business owner can act on.

## Why Monthly Beats Year-End

Some businesses genuinely do leave everything to year end. The cost shows up in three places.

First, you lose the chance to act. Margins slipping, a customer quietly going sixty days overdue, costs creeping: all visible monthly, all invisible until April otherwise.

Second, it costs more. Reconstructing twelve months of records under deadline pressure takes longer and produces a worse result than maintaining them as you go.

Third, it puts your filings at risk. The Corporate Tax return and payment are both due within nine months of year end, and the clock does not pause while your books are rebuilt.

Monthly closing means your year end is a summary of twelve finished months rather than a twelve-month project.

## Free Zone Companies Are Not Exempt

Being in a free zone does not remove the need for proper accounting, and this assumption costs companies real money.

A Qualifying Free Zone Person must maintain audited financial statements regardless of revenue, and the 0% rate on qualifying income depends on satisfying conditions. Demonstrating that you satisfy them requires precisely the records that disciplined bookkeeping produces. "We are in a free zone" is a structural fact, not a filing position.

## Where Technology Helps, and Where It Does Not

Modern accounting connects to the systems a business already runs: banks, payment gateways, e-commerce platforms, invoicing, CRM, payroll and expense tools.

A well-built workflow can run like this:

**Invoice received → data extracted → transaction categorised → accounting system updated → document stored → dashboard refreshed**

AI and automation handle invoice and receipt extraction, expense categorisation, transaction matching, payment reminders and reporting well. For a business processing hundreds of transactions a month, that removes most of the repetitive work.

What automation does not replace is control. Reconciliation, review, documentation and professional oversight still need to happen. An automated system that miscategorises consistently produces a clean-looking set of wrong books, which is harder to spot than obviously messy ones.

## The Mistakes That Cost Most

**Mixing personal and business spending.** The most common problem in owner-managed companies and the one that makes everything downstream harder.

**Skipping bank reconciliation.** Unreconciled accounts mean unreliable reports, and you will not know which figures are wrong.

**Missing supporting documents.** An entry without a document behind it is a weakness in an audit or an FTA review.

**Misclassifying VAT.** Zero-rated, exempt and standard-rated are not interchangeable, and corrections through voluntary disclosure cost more than getting it right.

**Ignoring receivables.** Profit on paper, no cash in the account.

**Treating bookkeeping as annual.** Covered above, and worth repeating.

**Spreadsheets past their useful life.** Fine for a handful of monthly transactions. Beyond that, the lack of audit trail and reconciliation becomes a liability.

## Choosing a Partner

Price is the easiest thing to compare and the least useful. Worth weighing instead:

- **UAE regulatory knowledge.** Do they understand Corporate Tax, VAT and FTA record requirements, not just bookkeeping generally?
- **Technology.** Can they work with cloud accounting and automation, or will you be emailing spreadsheets?
- **Reporting.** Will you receive management information you can act on, or only transaction entries?
- **Scalability.** Does the process survive your transaction volume tripling?
- **Data security.** How are your financial documents stored and accessed?
- **Responsiveness.** Can you get a current figure when you need it for a decision?

Most people arrive at company formation in Dubai with the same question: what does it cost? It is a fair question, and it is almost always the wrong one to start with. The cost follows from decisions you have not made yet, and making those decisions badly is far more expensive than any licence fee.

This guide walks through the whole process the way we would explain it across a table: what the structures actually mean, how to choose between them, what the money goes on, and where founders most often get it wrong.

## What Is Company Formation in Dubai?

Company formation in Dubai is the process of legally registering a business with a licensing authority so it can operate, hire, invoice and bank in its own name. In practice it means choosing your business activity and legal structure, selecting a jurisdiction, reserving a trade name, obtaining approvals, securing a licence and an address, then dealing with visas, banking and tax registration.

The sequence matters because each step constrains the next. Your activity determines which jurisdictions can license you. Your jurisdiction determines your premises and visa options. Those determine much of your cost.

Requirements vary by authority, activity and structure. There is no universal checklist, which is why generic advice about Dubai business setup tends to fall apart on contact with a specific business.

## Why Start a Business in Dubai?

Dubai works as a base for reasons that are practical rather than promotional.

It sits within reach of markets across the GCC, South Asia, Africa and Europe, with the air and sea connectivity to serve them. Government services are heavily digitised, so a good deal of the administrative process happens online. The workforce is international, which matters if you are hiring for skills rather than geography.

The business environment is genuinely varied: trading and logistics, professional services, technology, e-commerce, media, healthcare and manufacturing all have established footings here. Clusters such as Dubai Internet City, Dubai Silicon Oasis and DMCC have built communities around particular sectors, while Jebel Ali and Dubai South are oriented toward logistics and industry.

That variety is also why jurisdiction choice is not a formality. A structure that suits a consultancy in Business Bay is often wrong for a trading company moving containers through Jebel Ali.

## Types of Companies You Can Set Up in Dubai

### Mainland Company

A mainland company is licensed by Dubai's Department of Economy and Tourism and can trade throughout the UAE market and contract with government entities.

This suits businesses whose customers are UAE-based: consultancies selling to local firms, retail, food and beverage, clinics, contracting, and anyone bidding for public sector work. Premises requirements depend on the activity, and physical operations generally need real space with a registered tenancy. Foreign ownership is permitted across most commercial activities following the UAE's ownership reforms.

### Free Zone Company

A free zone is a designated economic area with its own registration authority, its own rules and its own incentive package. Dubai has many, and they are not interchangeable.

Entrepreneurs choose free zones for full foreign ownership, packaged pricing, sector-specific communities and flexible premises options. Many offer flexi-desk or shared-desk arrangements that satisfy the address requirement for service businesses, with visa allocations that vary by package and authority.

The consideration to understand upfront: doing business directly in the UAE mainland market from a free zone company can involve additional arrangements depending on your activity. If your revenue will come from UAE-based customers, test that assumption before you commit.

### Offshore Company

An offshore company is a corporate vehicle typically used for holding assets, owning shares in other companies or structuring international arrangements.

It is not a substitute for an operating licence. An offshore company does not give you the right to trade in the UAE market in the way a mainland or free zone licence does, and it does not carry the residence visa entitlements people sometimes assume. If your plan involves living in Dubai and serving customers here, offshore is the wrong tool.

## Mainland vs Free Zone Company Formation in Dubai

| | Mainland | Free Zone |
|---|---|---|
| **Ownership** | Foreign ownership permitted in most activities | Full foreign ownership |
| **UAE market access** | Trade freely across the UAE; can contract with government | Additional arrangements may apply for direct mainland trade |
| **Office** | Usually physical premises with registered tenancy | Flexi-desk or shared desk often sufficient, varies by zone |
| **Licensing** | Department of Economy and Tourism | Each free zone's own authority |
| **Visas** | Typically linked to premises and activity | Allocation varies by package and authority |
| **Cost** | Often higher initial outlay, more clearance steps | Packaged pricing, wide range across zones |
| **Suits** | UAE-focused services, retail, F&B, contracting, government work | Trading and logistics, holding structures, export-facing and international service businesses |

Neither is better in the abstract. The test is simple: where are your customers, and how do your goods or services physically move? Answer that honestly and the structure usually picks itself. What does not work is choosing a free zone for an attractive package and then discovering your actual revenue needs trading rights you do not have.

## Business Licences in Dubai

Licence categories broadly follow what you do:

- **Commercial licence** for trading, buying and selling goods, general trading and distribution
- **Professional licence** for services delivered on expertise: consultancy, IT services, marketing, design, legal and accounting practices
- **Industrial licence** for manufacturing, processing and assembly
- **Tourism-related licences** for travel, tour operation and related hospitality activities

E-commerce, technology and consultancy activities each have their own defined activity codes, and the specific combination you register determines what you can legally do. This is where first-time founders most often go wrong: the activity is a dropdown on a form, and it quietly sets your ownership position, your licensing route and sometimes your approvals.

## Steps for Company Formation in Dubai

**Step 1: Define your business activity.** Be precise. The activity you register governs everything downstream, and amending it later means changing your licence.

**Step 2: Select mainland or free zone.** Driven by your customers and operating model, not by package price.

**Step 3: Choose the legal structure.** LLC, sole establishment, branch or free zone entity, depending on shareholders, liability and jurisdiction.

**Step 4: Select and reserve a trade name.** Names must meet naming conventions and avoid restricted terms. Reserve early; it is cheap and blocks a common delay.

**Step 5: Obtain initial approval.** Confirmation in principle that the authority has no objection to your proposed activity and structure.

**Step 6: Prepare the documents.** Corporate or overseas shareholders need attested paperwork, and this consistently takes longer than founders budget for.

**Step 7: Arrange premises.** Flexi-desk, shared office or physical space with registered tenancy, depending on jurisdiction and activity. Your visa allocation often follows from this.

**Step 8: Obtain the licence.** Fees paid, documents accepted, licence issued. You are now a company.

**Step 9: Establishment and immigration files.** Registering with the labour and immigration systems, which is what allows you to sponsor anyone.

**Step 10: Apply for residence visas where eligible.** Entry permit, medical testing, Emirates ID and visa stamping for those who qualify.

**Step 11: Open a corporate bank account.** Start this earlier than feels necessary. See below.

**Step 12: Complete tax and compliance registration.** Corporate tax registration, VAT where thresholds are met, and the accounting records to support both.

## Documents Required for Company Formation in Dubai

Commonly requested:

- Passport copy for each shareholder and manager
- Passport-size photograph
- UAE entry stamp or visa page where applicable
- Emirates ID where the applicant is already resident
- Proof of address where required
- Business plan for certain regulated or higher-scrutiny activities
- No objection certificate where applicable
- Attested corporate documents where a shareholder is a company
- Memorandum and articles or the jurisdiction's equivalent

Requirements vary by authority, activity, shareholder structure and jurisdiction. Treat this as an orientation, not a definitive list, and confirm with your licensing authority.

## How Much Does Company Formation in Dubai Cost?

There is no single price, and the honest answer is that it depends on decisions you make. What you can do is understand the components.

**First-year setup typically includes:** initial approval, trade name reservation, licence and registration fees, premises or flexi-desk, establishment card and immigration file, then per-person visa costs covering entry permit, medical examination, Emirates ID and stamping.

**Recurring annual costs include:** licence renewal, premises renewal, visa renewals on their cycle, accounting and bookkeeping, audit where required, and tax filing.

Two points founders miss. First, the headline licence fee is a fraction of the total, and a low quote that excludes visas, premises and establishment costs is not a low price. Second, year two is not free. Ask for the renewal figure before you sign anything, not after.

Fees vary depending on the licensing authority, business activity, office requirements and visa package. Anyone giving you a firm number before asking about all four is quoting a package rather than pricing your business.

## Can Foreigners Own a Company in Dubai?

Yes, and in most activities you can own the whole thing.

Free zones have always permitted full foreign ownership. On the mainland, UAE reforms removed the general requirement for an Emirati shareholder across the majority of commercial activities, which changed the setup conversation considerably. A smaller set of activities remains restricted or subject to specific approvals.

A great deal of published material still states that every mainland company needs a local partner. That is out of date as a general rule. Confirm the position for your specific activity rather than relying on either the old rule or the new one.

## Dubai Company Formation and UAE Residence Visa

Forming a company is what makes visa sponsorship possible. It does not make it automatic.

Once your establishment and immigration files are open, the company can sponsor investor or partner visas for owners, employment visas for staff, and in turn those residents may sponsor family members subject to the applicable conditions.

Eligibility depends on immigration rules, company structure, premises and available quota. Each application is assessed individually, and no one can responsibly guarantee an outcome. Plan your visa requirements before you choose premises, because the two are frequently linked.

## Opening a Corporate Bank Account in Dubai

This is where otherwise smooth setups stall, and it deserves attention earlier than it usually gets.

Banks will want your trade licence and incorporation documents, shareholder and ultimate beneficial owner information, identification for signatories, a clear description of the business, expected transaction activity and source of funds. Many ask for a business plan.

The thing to understand is that a bank is assessing whether your business makes sense, not just whether your file is complete. Where do the funds come from, who ultimately owns this, where are your customers and suppliers, and does the stated activity match what you will actually do? Applications fail on an incoherent story far more often than on a missing form.

Company formation and account opening are separate processes with separate criteria. Holding a licence does not entitle you to an account, and no consultant can guarantee approval.

## Corporate Tax and VAT Considerations

**Corporate tax** applies at 0% on taxable income up to AED 375,000 and 9% above that. Registration and filing are required even where no tax is payable, and the return and the payment are both due within nine months of your financial year end.

**Small Business Relief** allows resident businesses with revenue of AED 3 million or less to elect to be treated as having no taxable income. This was due to end in 2026 and much published guidance still says so. Ministerial Decision No. 131 of 2026 extended it to tax periods ending on or before 31 December 2029. The test is on revenue rather than profit, and it must be elected in a filed return.

**VAT** is charged at 5%, with registration mandatory once taxable supplies and imports exceed AED 375,000 over twelve months and voluntary registration available from AED 187,500.

**Free zone companies** are not outside the system. A Qualifying Free Zone Person may access a 0% rate on qualifying income subject to conditions, but must still register, file and maintain audited financial statements regardless of revenue.

Tax rules change. Verify your specific position with the Federal Tax Authority or a qualified tax professional rather than relying on any article, including this one. ## How Long Does Company Formation in Dubai Take?

Timelines vary genuinely, so be wary of universal promises.

The licence itself can move quickly once activity, structure and documents are settled. What extends the process: attestation of foreign or corporate documents, external approvals triggered by particular activities, premises arrangements, immigration processing, and banking.

Banking is the step least within anyone's control. A company can be licensed in days and still be weeks from a working account. Where you see "company formation in 24 hours" advertised, read it as a specific packaged service for a narrow set of circumstances, not a description of the general process.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

1. **Choosing the wrong business activity.** It sets ownership, licensing and approvals, and changing it later means amending your licence.
2. **Selecting a jurisdiction on price alone.** The cheapest package is not cheap if it cannot serve your customers.
3. **Ignoring renewal costs.** Year two arrives regardless. Ask for the number upfront.
4. **Underestimating premises requirements.** Some activities need real space, and visa allocation often follows from it.
5. **Assuming all free zones work the same way.** They differ on activities, costs, facilities and standing with banks.
6. **Leaving banking until last.** Start preparing the file during formation, not after.
7. **Ignoring accounting and tax from day one.** Reconstructing a year of records under deadline is the most avoidable cost in this whole process.
8. **Misunderstanding visa eligibility.** Formation enables sponsorship; it does not guarantee visas for everyone involved.
9. **Choosing an unsuitable legal structure.** Shareholder arrangements are much harder to change afterwards.
10. **Skipping activity-specific approvals.** Some activities need regulator sign-off that no package price includes.

## Company Formation for Different Business Types

**IT and software companies** typically need a professional or technology activity, and clusters such as Dubai Internet City and Dubai Silicon Oasis are built around this sector.

**AI and technology startups** should pay particular attention to activity definitions, since emerging areas do not always map neatly onto existing codes.

**Consultancies** usually take a professional licence, and free zone structures often suit them well where clients are international.

**Trading companies** need commercial activities, and the choice between mainland and free zone turns on whether you sell into the UAE market or move goods through it.

**E-commerce businesses** need e-commerce activities specifically, plus payment gateway and logistics arrangements that should be planned alongside the licence.

**Marketing agencies** take professional or media-related activities, with some creative work requiring additional approvals.

**Professional services** such as accounting, legal and engineering frequently require qualification evidence and regulator approval beyond the standard licence.

**General trading** allows a broad range of goods under one licence and is priced accordingly.

## Should You Use a Company Formation Consultant?

Sometimes, and it would be self-serving to say always.

If you know your activity, your jurisdiction is obvious and your structure is simple, a competent formation agent will serve you perfectly well. Advice earns its cost when the decision is genuinely uncertain: multiple viable jurisdictions, an unusual ownership chain, activities needing regulatory approval, difficult banking, or a plan to expand across the GCC.

When comparing providers, compare like for like: government fees versus service fees, what renewal costs, whether visas and premises are included, and what happens after the licence is issued. A quote that looks materially cheaper usually excludes something.

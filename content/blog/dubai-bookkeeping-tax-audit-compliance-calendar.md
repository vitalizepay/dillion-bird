The conversation we have most often in September goes like this. A business owner realises their corporate tax return is due at the end of the month, calls to ask how long it takes, and discovers that the honest answer depends entirely on a question they were not expecting: are your books actually finished?

Usually they are not. What follows is four weeks of reconstructing a year of transactions under deadline pressure, which costs more, produces a worse result, and is entirely avoidable.

Bookkeeping, tax and audit get treated as three separate services bought from three directions. They are one chain. Your books produce your financial statements. Your financial statements produce your tax return and, above certain thresholds, your audit. Weakness at the start of that chain shows up as cost and risk at the end of it, every time.

So this is the compliance picture for a Dubai business in 2026: what is due, when, who it applies to, and what has changed recently enough that most published guidance has not caught up.

## The 2026 Compliance Calendar at a Glance

| Obligation | Who it applies to | Deadline |
|---|---|---|
| Corporate tax registration | All taxable persons | Before your first return is due |
| Corporate tax return and payment | All taxable persons | Within 9 months of financial year end |
| Small Business Relief election | Revenue up to AED 3m, if electing | In the return, within 9 months of year end |
| VAT registration | Taxable supplies over AED 375,000 | Within 30 days of exceeding the threshold |
| VAT return and payment | VAT-registered businesses | Within 28 days of each tax period end |
| Audited financial statements | Revenue over AED 50m, and all QFZPs | Alongside the corporate tax return |
| E-invoicing readiness | Revenue AED 50m or more | Go live from 1 January 2027 |
| E-invoicing readiness | Revenue under AED 50m | Go live from 1 July 2027 |
| Accounting record retention | All businesses | Keep for 7 years |

Two dates on that list do most of the damage when missed: the nine-month corporate tax deadline, because payment falls due the same day rather than later, and the VAT 28-day window, because it recurs and small habitual delays accumulate into a compliance history that is difficult to explain later.

## Corporate Tax: What You Owe and When

UAE corporate tax is charged at **0% on taxable income up to AED 375,000** and **9% above it**.

The return and the payment are both due **within nine months of your financial year end**. If your year end is 31 December 2026, that means 30 September 2027. If it is 30 June 2027, that means 31 March 2028. Businesses consistently plan for the filing and forget that the cash goes out on the same date.

Registration is separate from filing and comes first. Every taxable person must be registered with the Federal Tax Authority, including businesses that expect to pay nothing because they fall under the threshold or qualify for relief. Owing no tax is not the same as having no obligation, and this is the single most common misunderstanding we correct.

Free zone companies are not outside the system. A Qualifying Free Zone Person may access a 0% rate on qualifying income if it meets the substance and other conditions, but it still registers, still files, and still has to maintain audited financial statements whatever its revenue. "We are in a free zone" is not a compliance position.

## Small Business Relief Now Runs to 2029

This is the update worth knowing about, because most articles you will find still state the old position.

**Ministerial Decision No. 131 of 2026, issued on 29 July 2026, extended Small Business Relief to tax periods ending on or before 31 December 2029.** The original sunset was 31 December 2026. That is a three-year extension, announced roughly a month before this article was written, and a great deal of guidance published earlier still says the relief is about to expire.

The mechanics are unchanged. A resident business with **revenue of AED 3 million or less** can elect to be treated as having no taxable income for that period, which reduces corporate tax to zero and simplifies compliance considerably. The AED 3 million threshold from Ministerial Decision No. 73 of 2023 stays as it is.

Two things to be careful about. The test is on **revenue**, not profit, so a business turning over AED 4 million at a loss does not qualify. And the relief must be **elected in a filed return**. It does not apply automatically because you are under the threshold. Businesses that assume it is automatic and skip the return get the worst of both outcomes: no relief and a late filing.

If you were planning your next three years on the assumption that relief disappears at the end of 2026, that plan is now worth revisiting.

## VAT: The Recurring One

VAT is 5%. Registration becomes mandatory once taxable supplies and imports exceed **AED 375,000** over a twelve-month period, and voluntary registration is available from **AED 187,500**.

Returns are filed through EmaraTax and are due **within 28 days of the end of each tax period**, quarterly for most businesses and monthly for larger ones. Payment accompanies the return.

VAT is where bookkeeping quality shows up fastest. A quarterly return is only as good as the three months of records behind it, and a business reconciling its bank at the end of each month files accurately in a morning. A business that reconciles at the deadline files late, or files something it later has to correct. Voluntary disclosures to fix earlier returns are more expensive and more visible than getting it right the first time.

## Who Actually Needs an Audit

More businesses than realise it, and for more reasons than tax.

Under the corporate tax rules, audited financial statements are required where **annual revenue exceeds AED 50 million**, and for **all Qualifying Free Zone Persons regardless of revenue**. The AED 50 million threshold is not prorated, so a nine-month tax period that crosses it still triggers the requirement.

Tax law is not the only source of an audit requirement, though. Many free zone authorities require audited accounts as a licence renewal condition irrespective of your tax position. Banks routinely ask for them before extending or renewing facilities. Investors and acquirers expect them. Shareholder agreements often mandate them. A business well under AED 50 million in revenue can easily still need an audit for three of those reasons at once.

The practical consequence is worth stating plainly: if there is any chance you will need an audit, keep your records to audit standard from the start of the year. Retrofitting audit-quality documentation onto a year that was casually recorded is the most expensive way to arrive at the same result.

## E-Invoicing Is Closer Than It Looks

The UAE is moving to structured electronic invoicing on a phased timetable.

A voluntary pilot opened on **1 July 2026**. Businesses with revenue of **AED 50 million or more go live from 1 January 2027**. Businesses **below AED 50 million follow on 1 July 2027**, and government entities from **1 October 2027**.

Those dates sound comfortable. They are not, because the work is not the go-live, it is the system. E-invoicing requires your accounting platform to issue and receive invoices in a structured format that integrates with the FTA's framework. If you are running your accounts on spreadsheets, or on software that will not support it, the change is a migration rather than a setting, and migrations done in the last quarter before a mandatory deadline go badly.

The useful thing to do in 2026 is find out whether your current system will support e-invoicing at your phase date. If the answer is no, you have time to change it deliberately. Businesses that ask this question in 2027 will not.

## What Good Bookkeeping Actually Looks Like

None of the above is difficult if the foundation is right. In practice, that means a small number of unglamorous habits.

Reconcile bank accounts monthly, not quarterly and certainly not annually. Keep business and personal spending genuinely separate, which is the single most common problem in owner-managed companies here and the one that makes everything downstream harder. Retain source documents rather than just the ledger entries, because the record retention requirement is seven years and an entry without a document behind it is a weakness in an audit or an FTA review. Record VAT correctly at the point of entry rather than fixing it at the return. And close each month properly, so that the year end is a summary of twelve completed months rather than a twelve-month project.

Businesses that do these things find tax season uneventful. That is the entire goal. Compliance done well is not visible.

## Where It Goes Wrong

**Registering late, or not at all, because no tax is owed.** Registration and liability are separate obligations.

**Treating Small Business Relief as automatic.** It requires an election in a filed return.

**Discovering the audit requirement after year end.** By then the year's records are what they are.

**Missing that payment is due with the corporate tax return.** Nine months out, not later, and it should be in the cash flow forecast from the start of the year.

**Assuming free zone status removes the obligations.** It changes the rate that may apply. It does not remove registration, filing, or, for QFZPs, the audit requirement.

**Leaving e-invoicing until the phase date.** It is a systems question, and systems questions need lead time.

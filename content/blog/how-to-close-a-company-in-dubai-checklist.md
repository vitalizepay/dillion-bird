Most people come to us at the wrong point in this process. Not at the start, when closing a Dubai company is a manageable administrative exercise, but eight months in, holding a stack of half-finished paperwork, an expired licence accruing fines, and one employee whose visa nobody remembered to cancel.

The frustrating part is that closure in Dubai is not complicated. It is sequential. Every step depends on the one before it, and the authorities check in a specific order. Miss a document early and you do not find out at that step. You find out three steps later, when someone declines your file and you start again.

So this is the checklist we work from internally, in the order the authorities actually ask for things. If you want the legal background instead - what changed under the new bankruptcy law, how insolvency differs from liquidation, where directors carry personal exposure - that is covered in our [Dubai liquidation and insolvency guide](/blog/dubai-company-liquidation-insolvency-guide). This piece is the practical companion to it: what to gather, in what order, and what will trip you up.

## First, Answer Three Questions

Before touching a form, settle these. Getting any of them wrong means restarting.

**Which authority licensed the company?** Dubai mainland companies answer to the Department of Economy and Tourism. Free zone entities answer to their own registration authority, and DMCC, JAFZA, DAFZA, Meydan and IFZA each run their own procedure. DIFC entities sit under DIFC Insolvency Law and the DIFC Courts, which is a different legal exercise entirely. There is no universal Dubai closure process, and assuming otherwise is the most common structural mistake.

**Can the company pay everything it owes?** If yes, this is a voluntary closure and the checklist below applies. If no, stop. An insolvent company cannot simply be wound down by settling whoever shouts loudest. Once there is not enough to go round, the law decides the order of payment, not the shareholders, and choosing your own order is how a corporate problem becomes a personal one.

**Are the accounts current?** Not roughly current. Current. The liquidator cannot produce the mandatory audit report without them, and the Federal Tax Authority will not issue clearance while filings are outstanding. If the books are two years behind, that is the real first task, whatever the plan says.

## Phase 1: Resolution and Appointment

- Board or shareholder resolution to dissolve the company and appoint a liquidator, notarised. Corporate or overseas shareholders will need attested documents, which takes longer than people budget for.
- Appointment of a registered liquidator and their formal letter of acceptance. This letter is part of the filing bundle, not a formality to chase later.
- Original trade licence, memorandum of association and any amendments.
- Passport copies, Emirates IDs and visa copies for all shareholders and managers.
- Power of attorney, if anyone is signing on behalf of a shareholder.

Filed together, these move the company to "Under Liquidation" on the commercial register. Worth being clear about what that status means: the company still exists. It still has obligations. Nothing is closed yet.

## Phase 2: The Creditor Notice

- Liquidation certificate issued by the authority after the Phase 1 filing.
- Notice published in two local newspapers, one Arabic and one English, for mainland companies.
- **A minimum of 45 days** for creditors to come forward, counted from publication.
- Copies of both published notices retained for the final file.

The 45 days cannot be shortened, so treat it as free working time rather than a pause. Everything in Phase 3 can and should run inside it. Companies that wait for the notice period to end before starting on clearances add two months to their own timeline for no reason.

## Phase 3: Clearances and Settlements

This is the phase that consumes the calendar. Each item is a separate application to a separate body, each on its own schedule, and several depend on each other.

**People**

- Cancel every residence visa on the company's file, including shareholders, employees and dependants.
- Settle end-of-service gratuity, notice pay, unpaid wages and accrued leave. This is not a negotiation to be won at closure. Unresolved employee claims will stop the process.
- Close the MOHRE labour file.
- Cancel the establishment immigration card, which can only happen once the last visa is gone.

**Premises and services**

- Final DEWA bill settled and clearance obtained.
- Telecom accounts with Etisalat or du closed and cleared.
- Tenancy terminated, Ejari cancelled, and a no-objection letter from the landlord.
- P.O. Box closed and mail redirected.
- Chilled water, district cooling and building service accounts, which are billed separately from DEWA and are routinely forgotten.

**Operations**

- Customs code cancelled, if the company held one.
- RTA and vehicle registrations transferred or cancelled, if any are in the company name.
- Sector permits and regulatory registrations surrendered, if the activity required them.

**Banking**

- Corporate bank accounts formally closed, with a bank closure letter on letterhead. Banks are not fast at this, and the letter is a required document later, so start it early rather than at the end.
- Standing instructions, corporate cards, trade facilities and cheque books cancelled and returned.
- Merchant and payment gateway accounts terminated.

## Phase 4: Tax

Handle this in parallel with Phase 3, not after it. The deadlines run from the date the business ceases trading, not from the date the licence is finally cancelled, and this mismatch is where most penalties are earned.

- **Corporate tax deregistration within three months** of cessation, dissolution or liquidation, via EmaraTax. Late application draws a penalty of AED 1,000, then a further AED 1,000 every month on the same date, capped at AED 10,000. The FTA reviews within 30 business days.
- **VAT deregistration within 20 business days** of ceasing taxable supplies, if registered. Separate application, separate clock.
- All outstanding VAT and corporate tax returns filed and any liability paid.
- **FTA tax clearance certificate obtained.** This is the one to watch. It confirms nothing is outstanding, and it has become the practical gate on the whole closure. Neither DET nor a free zone authority will finalise deregistration without it, so a single unfiled return from an earlier period can hold the entire company open.

## Phase 5: Final Deregistration

- Liquidator's final audit report, covering the period from the last audited year end through to the liquidation date and confirming assets, liabilities and shareholder equity at closure. This is a distinct document from your annual audit, and it is mandatory for both mainland and free zone closures.
- Declaration from the liquidator and shareholders confirming no creditor objections were received during the notice period.
- Complete clearance pack from Phase 3.
- FTA tax clearance certificate.
- Bank closure letter.
- Original licence and establishment card surrendered.
- Final fees paid and the deregistration certificate issued.

Keep the deregistration certificate somewhere permanent. It is the only proof the company was closed properly, and it is the document people need years later when a bank or an authority asks what happened to the entity.

## The Consolidated Checklist

Working order, start to finish:

1. Confirm licensing authority and applicable regime
2. Confirm solvency, honestly
3. Bring accounts fully up to date
4. Pass and notarise the dissolution resolution
5. Appoint the liquidator and obtain the letter of acceptance
6. File with the authority and obtain the liquidation certificate
7. Publish notices in Arabic and English newspapers
8. Start the 45-day creditor period
9. Cancel all visas, settle staff, close the labour file
10. Cancel the establishment immigration card
11. Clear DEWA, telecoms, cooling and the P.O. Box
12. Terminate the tenancy, cancel Ejari, obtain the landlord NOC
13. Cancel customs code, vehicles and sector permits
14. Close bank accounts and obtain the closure letter
15. File all outstanding tax returns
16. Apply for VAT deregistration, within 20 business days of ceasing supplies
17. Apply for corporate tax deregistration, within three months of cessation
18. Obtain the FTA tax clearance certificate
19. Obtain the liquidator's final audit report
20. Submit the complete pack and collect the deregistration certificate

## Where Free Zones Differ

The shape is the same. The detail is not.

Free zone authorities set their own notice periods, forms and sequencing, and some require a liquidator from their own approved panel rather than one of your choosing. Two things hold almost everywhere: the liquidation audit report is required just as it is on the mainland, and no authority will release final deregistration while a single visa remains active on the establishment file.

Do not work from a general checklist, including this one, without confirming the current requirements with your specific authority. Free zone procedures are updated more often than published guides are.

## What Actually Goes Wrong

Four things, in order of how often we see them.

Accounts that were never properly maintained. The liquidator cannot produce the audit report, the FTA will not clear the file, and what looked like a three-month closure becomes a bookkeeping reconstruction project first. This one is entirely preventable and almost never is.

Visas cancelled too late. Everything downstream waits, because the immigration card cannot close while a visa is live, and deregistration cannot proceed while the card is open.

Tax deregistration left until the licence is cancelled. The three-month corporate tax clock started when the business stopped trading. By the time the licence goes, the penalty has often been accruing for months.

Bank closure started last. It is treated as an afterthought, the letter is a required document, and banks work to their own timetable regardless of yours.

## A Note on Timing

You will see closure timelines quoted anywhere from 45 working days to six months. Both are true, for different files.

The fixed elements are short. The 45-day notice period is the only truly immovable block, and the administrative filings are quick when the paperwork is right. What stretches a closure is everything that should have been in order before it started: the accounts, the filings, the visas, the disputes nobody resolved.

For a mainland LLC in reasonable order, plan on three to six months. For a company with clean books, no staff and no disputes, considerably less. For one with two years of unfiled returns, the honest answer is that the closure has not started yet. The bookkeeping has.

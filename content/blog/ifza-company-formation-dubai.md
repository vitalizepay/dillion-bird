Most enquiries about free zone company formation start in the wrong place: which package is cheapest. The package is the last decision, not the first, and getting there in the wrong order is how founders end up with a licence that cannot do what their business needs.

This guide covers IFZA specifically, what a free zone setup through it actually involves, and the structural questions worth settling first. For the broader picture including mainland options, see our [complete guide to company formation in Dubai](/blog/company-formation-in-dubai).

## What IFZA Is

IFZA, the International Free Zone Authority, is a Dubai free zone offering company formation and business licensing, operating from Dubai Silicon Oasis through a partner-led model. It suits micro, small and medium enterprises as well as international businesses establishing a UAE presence.

What it offers, on its own published terms:

- **100% foreign ownership**, with no local partner or sponsor required, and full repatriation of capital and profits
- **Commercial, professional and industrial** activity categories, plus branch registration for eligible foreign-incorporated companies
- **Multiple activities under one licence**, subject to its activity rules, including combinations across categories in some cases
- **Remote incorporation**, with documents submitted and signed digitally and the licence issued electronically
- **Flexible workspace solutions**, including virtual workplace options for eligible businesses

Registration is commonly completed within roughly three to seven working days once documentation is in order, with no minimum share capital requirement.

## Choosing the Licence

The licence follows the activity, and the activity follows what you actually sell.

A **professional licence** generally suits consultancy and service businesses: management consultancy, technical and IT services, digital services, marketing and similar professional work.

A **commercial licence** covers trading activity: general trading, e-commerce, import and export, distribution and related retail activities.

**Branch registration** is the route for an existing foreign company extending into the UAE rather than incorporating something new.

The mistake worth avoiding is picking the cheapest package and retrofitting your business into it. A company licensed for an unrelated trading activity while actually selling software consultancy creates problems at the bank, at renewal, and anywhere the licence is examined.

## Combining Activities

IFZA's flexibility on combining activities under a single licence is genuinely useful for hybrid businesses. A firm doing both management consultancy and IT services, or a business that consults and also resells software, can often sit under one licence rather than two.

The caveat: combinations are subject to the authority's rules and fees, and not every pair of activities can be grouped. Confirm the exact combination before incorporating, because changing it afterwards means amending the licence.

## What Remote Setup Does and Does Not Cover

Remote incorporation is real and useful. Documents are handled digitally, signatures are electronic, and the licence arrives by email.

What it does not cover is everything after the licence. The visa process requires you in the UAE for medical testing and Emirates ID biometrics. Most banks want to meet signatories in person before opening an account. So a founder abroad can genuinely form the company remotely, but should plan at least one trip to finish the job.

Worth setting expectations on this early, because "set up your Dubai company without leaving home" is frequently read as covering the whole journey.

## The Free Zone Tax Question

This is the misunderstanding we correct most often.

A free zone company is not outside the UAE corporate tax system. A Qualifying Free Zone Person may access a 0% rate on qualifying income where the conditions are satisfied, but it still registers for corporate tax, still files returns, and must maintain audited financial statements regardless of revenue. Income that does not meet the qualifying conditions is taxed under the normal rules.

"We are in a free zone" is a structural fact, not a tax position. Build your setup with registration, filing and accounting obligations in view from the start, and confirm your specific position with the Federal Tax Authority or a qualified tax advisor. ## Banking Comes Earlier Than You Think

A trade licence does not entitle you to a corporate bank account. Banks assess ownership, source of funds, business model, expected transaction volumes, and where your customers and suppliers are based.

The determining factor is usually coherence rather than paperwork. Does the stated activity match what the business will actually do? Does the ownership chain make sense? Can you explain where the money comes from? Applications stall on an unconvincing narrative far more often than on a missing document.

Prepare the corporate profile during formation rather than after it: business description, expected revenue and transaction activity, customer and supplier geography, website, contracts or invoices where available, and shareholder information.

## Free Zone or Mainland

Neither is universally right, and anyone telling you otherwise without asking about your customers is selling.

A **free zone structure** suits businesses serving international clients, trading and holding arrangements, and service firms that do not need broad direct access to the UAE domestic market. Full foreign ownership, packaged pricing, flexible workspace and streamlined incorporation are the practical draws.

A **mainland licence** suits businesses selling to UAE-based customers, operating retail or physical premises, or contracting with government entities.

The test: where are your customers, and how do your goods or services actually move? Answer honestly and the structure usually picks itself. What does not work is choosing a free zone for the package price and then discovering your revenue depends on market access you do not have.

## Who This Suits

IFZA works well for consultancies and professional services firms, technology and software businesses, e-commerce and digital businesses, trading companies, and overseas founders establishing a first UAE presence.

It works less well where the business needs broad direct access to the UAE domestic market, requires substantial physical premises, or operates an activity needing regulatory approval beyond standard licensing.

For technology founders in particular, the licence is the smallest part. The question is not how to obtain a UAE licence. It is what structure lets you build, sell and scale across the GCC, which pulls in banking, contracts, data considerations, hiring and market entry. That is the conversation worth having first.

## Before You Pick a Package

Settle these before comparing prices:

1. What exactly will the company sell, and to whom?
2. Are your customers inside or outside the UAE?
3. Services, goods, or both?
4. Do you need visas, and how many?
5. Do you need physical premises?
6. Does your activity need external approval?
7. What will year two cost, not just year one?

A quote that looks materially cheaper usually excludes something. Compare government fees against service fees, check what renewal costs, and confirm whether visas and workspace are included.

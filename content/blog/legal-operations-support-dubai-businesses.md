A dispute rarely arrives as a clean legal question. It arrives as a contract somewhere in a shared drive, eighteen months of email, a WhatsApp thread on someone's personal phone, invoices in the accounting system, and a site photograph that may or may not still exist.

The first real problem is not finding a lawyer. It is working out what actually happened and proving it.

This piece covers what UAE law says about digital evidence, how to organise a dispute file properly, and where technology genuinely helps. One thing first, because it governs everything below: **Dillon & Bird is not a law firm.** We do not provide legal advice, legal opinions or court representation, and we do not hold ourselves out as able to. Those belong with an appropriately licensed advocate or legal consultant. What we do is the business, information and technology work around a matter, alongside your legal team.

## Your WhatsApp Messages Probably Are Evidence

This surprises people, and it cuts both ways.

**Federal Decree-Law No. 35 of 2022** on Evidence in Civil and Commercial Transactions contains a dedicated chapter on electronic evidence. Two provisions matter commercially:

**Article 53** gives electronic documents the same legal effect as written ones, where their **origin and integrity can be verified**.

**Article 54** allows reliance on electronic messages, including emails, where the **sender's identity and the recipient's receipt** can be established.

So the email chain where your client approved the extra work counts. So does the WhatsApp thread where your project manager agreed to a variation nobody formally documented.

The conditions are where businesses lose. "Same effect as written evidence, if origin and integrity can be verified" is not the same as "a screenshot is proof". A message forwarded between phones, cropped, re-saved and pasted into a document has lost most of what made it verifiable. The same exchange preserved as a proper export, with metadata intact and a clear chain of custody, is a different proposition entirely.

Which makes this an information management problem with legal consequences, and that is squarely something a business can get right in advance.

## When a Notice Arrives

Receiving a legal notice does not mean you have lost. What you do in the first week matters more than most people assume.

**Preserve, and change nothing.** Do not delete, edit, re-save or tidy anything connected to the matter. That includes personal devices where business communication happened. The instinct to clean up is understandable and it is the worst available move.

**Collect across every location.** In practice material sits in email, WhatsApp, Google Drive or OneDrive, laptops, the accounting system or ERP, paper files and phones. Anything you miss now you will be hunting for under deadline later.

**Build a chronology.** Contract signed, work started, invoice issued, payment due, dispute raised, notice received. A clear factual timeline is the single most useful thing you can hand a lawyer.

**Separate four things:** what you know, what you believe, what you can prove, and what is missing. The gap between the second and third is where cases are won or lost, and it is better to find it now.

**Engage a licensed professional.** Dubai's Legal Affairs Department maintains a directory of registered advocates, legal consultants and licensed firms. Legal strategy, applicable law, claims, defences and filings are theirs to determine.

## Why Disputes Cost More Than They Should

A client once handed us roughly four thousand files for a commercial matter: one contract, fourteen invoices, several hundred emails, dozens of WhatsApp threads, photographs, meeting notes and payment records, in no particular order.

Their lawyer was entirely capable of working through it. The question was whether that was a sensible use of a senior lawyer's hourly rate.

This is the recurring pattern. The organising work has to happen regardless. The only real decision is who does it, at what rate, and whether the legal team starts on strategy or on sorting.

## What a Structured Case File Looks Like

A workable structure, which is deliberately unsophisticated:

- **01 Contract** - master agreement, amendments, purchase orders
- **02 Financial** - invoices, receipts, bank records, payment demands
- **03 Communications** - email, message exports, letters, meeting records
- **04 Project** - technical documents, drawings, photographs, delivery records
- **05 Legal** - notices, responses, court documents, correspondence with counsel

Alongside it, a chronology table: date, event, supporting evidence, business impact. Then a short case pack covering the parties, a factual summary, financial exposure, the evidence list, open questions, missing documents, and the specific points needing legal assessment.

None of this interprets the law. It gives your lawyer a factual foundation so the first meeting is about strategy.

## Where Technology Actually Helps

For a dispute with thousands of documents, manual review is slow and inconsistent.

AI-assisted processing handles the volume well: extracting dates, parties, invoice numbers and amounts, classifying documents, finding duplicates, surfacing correspondence around terms like approval, variation, delay, termination or settlement, flagging gaps, and assembling a draft chronology.

What it must not do is decide what any of it means. The workflow that works:

**AI identifies → a human reviews → the licensed legal professional evaluates legal significance**

Any tool or provider suggesting AI can assess your prospects of success is selling something that should not be bought. It processes information. It does not practise law.

## Private AI for Sensitive Material

Case files contain contracts, employee records, financial data and privileged correspondence. Pushing that into a public AI service is a decision with consequences, and for many businesses it is simply not acceptable.

A private deployment keeps processing inside infrastructure you control: secure document store, OCR and text extraction, classification, then search, summarisation and timeline building within that boundary, with access controls and audit trails throughout. Human review sits between the system and the legal team at every point.

The principle worth holding onto: **AI assists, humans decide, lawyers advise.**

## Doing This Before a Dispute

Everything above is cheaper as prevention.

Businesses that maintain organised contract records, consistent file naming, preserved metadata, searchable archives, sensible access controls and reliable backups are in a materially stronger position the day something goes wrong. They can establish origin and integrity because the records were kept properly, not reconstructed afterwards.

The same discipline has ordinary commercial value: knowing when contracts expire, when notice periods bite, what the penalty clauses say and which obligations are coming due. Most businesses find out what their contracts contain at precisely the wrong moment.

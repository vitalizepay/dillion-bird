Oman's banking sector is in the middle of a genuine shift. Traditional banking remains the backbone of the economy, but what is being built around it now involves digital banking, fintech, open banking, real-time payments, cloud infrastructure, data analytics and cybersecurity working together.

For businesses operating in Oman, investors entering the market, and the banks and technology firms serving them, the direction is worth understanding early.

## The Regulatory Picture

The Central Bank of Oman is the principal regulator, with the Financial Services Authority overseeing areas of the financial market within its mandate. Between them the framework covers banking operations, payment systems, AML and counter-terrorist financing, Islamic banking, digital banking and open banking.

The significant recent change is the **Banking Law under Royal Decree No. 2 of 2025**, enacted on 1 January 2025. It repealed and replaced the previous law from Royal Decree 114 of 2000, modernising the framework and adding provisions for digital banking, Islamic banking and investment banking. A companion decree, Royal Decree 3/2025, restructured the Central Bank's own governance, replacing the Executive President role with a Governor.

For anyone planning banking, financing or fintech activity in Oman, that is the current foundation to build on.

## What Digital Transformation Actually Means Here

It is no longer about moving services from a branch into an app. Modern banking transformation pulls together cloud infrastructure, artificial intelligence, data analytics, API-based banking, cybersecurity, digital identity and electronic KYC, automation, real-time payments, fraud detection, regulatory technology and digital lending.

The Central Bank has developed policies covering cloud computing, cybersecurity and resilience, digital onboarding and eKYC, payment services, card tokenisation and open banking. That gives institutions room to modernise while keeping regulatory and security controls intact.

The goal is not more technology. It is a banking environment that is more efficient, more secure, more scalable and easier for customers to deal with.

## Open Banking

Open banking lets customers share financial data securely with authorised third parties through APIs, under consent and applicable regulation. The Central Bank's Open Banking Regulatory Framework places heavy emphasis on data and information security, technology risk management and cybersecurity across the ecosystem.

For businesses, it opens up better financial management tools, integrated accounting, automated payments, cash-flow visibility, faster onboarding and data aggregation.

For banks, it adds responsibility. API security, authentication, consent management, data governance, third-party risk and continuous monitoring all move into scope. Treating open banking as an API development project is the common mistake. It is equally a security, governance and compliance programme.

## FinTech and the Sandbox

Oman's fintech strategy focuses on economic growth, financial integrity, financial inclusion, support for startups and SMEs, cashless payments and investment in the ecosystem. The Central Bank and the Financial Services Authority operate a co-regulatory sandbox, giving firms a controlled environment to test new financial products.

That control matters. A banking application can affect customer funds, financial data, identity information and regulatory obligations in ways ordinary software does not, so understanding the risk before wide release is the point of the exercise.

Active areas include payments, digital lending, financial inclusion, Islamic fintech, RegTech, wealth technology, financial analytics, digital identity, compliance automation and embedded finance.

## Where AI Fits

AI has real application across banking: customer service assistants, fraud detection through transaction pattern analysis, credit risk analytics, AML transaction monitoring, intelligent document processing for statements and contracts, personalisation, and back-office automation.

It also needs governance. Data quality, privacy, explainability, security, model risk and human oversight are not optional in a regulated institution.

The question worth asking is not "where can we use AI?" It is "where does AI create measurable value while meeting regulatory, security and risk requirements?"

## Cybersecurity Is Not a Side Project

A modern banking estate spans mobile apps, internet banking, APIs, cloud services, third-party platforms, payment systems and automated processes. Every integration adds attack surface, which is why the Central Bank's open banking framework foregrounds technology risk management and cybersecurity.

The practical scope: identity and access management, multi-factor and privileged access, API security, encryption, security monitoring, vulnerability management, incident response, business continuity and disaster recovery, data protection, third-party risk and regular testing.

Security belongs in the architecture, development, operations and governance of a platform, not in a separate workstream that reviews it afterwards.

## Cloud, Assessed Properly

Cloud and hybrid architectures bring scalability and automation when implemented inside regulatory and security requirements. Kubernetes, OpenShift, containerisation, API gateways and automated DevSecOps pipelines all support modern banking platforms.

But workloads should not move because cloud is considered modern. Each one deserves assessment against data classification, regulatory requirements, security, performance, availability, integration dependencies, cost and operational maturity.

The question is not cloud or no cloud. It is what architecture is right for each workload, and the honest answer is usually a mix of on-premise, private and public.

## Payments

Oman has modernised payment infrastructure through 24/7 real-time gross settlement, IBAN, card tokenisation, e-mandates and direct debit enhancements.

For businesses that supports faster transactions, automated collections, digital commerce, subscription billing, cross-border payments, treasury management and cleaner reconciliation. For banks it raises the bar on availability, fraud control and compliance at the same time.

## Islamic Banking and Technology

Islamic banking is a substantial part of the system rather than a niche. Combined assets of Islamic banks and the Islamic windows of conventional banks reached roughly **RO 9.8 billion by the end of May 2026**, up about 9.2% year on year and close to **19% of total banking sector assets**.

That creates room for technology built specifically around Sharia-compliant products: digital onboarding, automated compliance workflows, Islamic financing platforms, financial education tools and digital investment services. Technology should support Sharia governance and regulatory oversight, not work around it.

## What This Means for Omani Businesses

Transformation is not only a bank-side story. Companies gain when banking connects to the systems they already run:

**ERP → banking APIs → payments → accounting → reporting → analytics**

That chain can automate invoice generation, payment requests, bank transaction collection, reconciliation, accounting updates, cash-flow reporting and management dashboards. For SMEs especially, the benefit is less administrative work and a clearer view of cash, which is usually worth more than the technology itself.

## Choosing a Technology Partner

Banking technology needs more than development capability. Worth assessing:

- **Regulatory understanding**, and willingness to work with your compliance and legal teams
- **Security** designed in from architecture through to operations
- **Integration** with existing ERP, CRM, payment, identity and financial systems
- **Scalability** for future transaction volumes
- **Data governance** across access, storage, processing and retention
- **Measurable outcomes**: processing time, cost, transaction volume, error rates, customer satisfaction, revenue impact

If a project cannot state what it will improve and by how much, that is worth resolving before it starts.

## A Practical Sequence

Transformation touches technology, finance, operations, risk, compliance and strategy at once, which is why it benefits from being staged rather than attempted wholesale:

1. Assess the current state of applications, infrastructure and processes
2. Identify where automation, AI, cloud or integration create measurable value
3. Build a phased roadmap aligned to business priorities
4. Design the target architecture, integration model and security controls
5. Implement and integrate
6. Validate against regulatory and security requirements
7. Measure the outcomes

This is mostly what prevents transformation becoming a set of disconnected projects.

## Where It Is Heading

Digital banking, open banking, AI, fintech, cybersecurity, hybrid cloud, real-time payments, Islamic fintech and RegTech will each keep shaping the sector. None of this replaces traditional banking. The branch, the app, the cloud platform, the API ecosystem, the AI engine, the payment network and the banker become components of one financial system.

The transformations that work will not be the ones that deploy the most technology. They will be the ones that improve customer experience, operational efficiency, security, compliance, financial inclusion and growth in ways someone can actually measure.
